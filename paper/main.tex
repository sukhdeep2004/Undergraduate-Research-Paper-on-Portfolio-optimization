\title{\bfseries A Design-Optimality Regularizer for Portfolio Selection:\\
  A-, D-, and E-Optimal Criteria as Structured Shrinkage}
\author{Sukhdeep Singh \and Saumen Mandal}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Global minimum-variance (GMV) portfolios are notoriously unstable: they invert a
noisy covariance estimate, so small estimation errors are amplified into large,
concentrated, high-turnover weights. We propose a unified, convex portfolio
optimizer that adds to the Markowitz risk objective a family of penalties
borrowed from \emph{optimal experimental design}. Reading the portfolio weights
as a design measure over a factor regression, we define a weight-dependent
information matrix $\M=B^\top\diag(\w)B$ and regularize the portfolio through the
classical A-, D-, and E-optimality criteria of that matrix. The three criteria
correspond to distinct, economically interpretable goals---minimum average
factor-estimation variance (A), balanced factor coverage and diversification (D),
and worst-case robustness (E). We develop the theory of the resulting program:
we characterize how the criteria depend on the choice of the design matrix
(establishing that the D-criterion is invariant to the factor basis while the A-
and E-criteria are not), give a Cauchy--Binet representation of the D-criterion
in the rank-deficient case $K<N$, and prove convexity, existence, uniqueness,
exact recovery of the GMV and ridge-GMV portfolios, monotone comparative statics
in the penalty strengths, and limiting solutions. A stability theorem shows that
the penalties increase the strong-convexity modulus of the objective and thereby
provably damp the sensitivity of the weights to covariance perturbations, giving
a theoretical explanation of the turnover reductions we observe. Empirically,
each criterion moves its own target quantity monotonically; in a high-dimensional
simulation ($N/T\to1$) the design penalties keep the weights stable where the
sample-covariance Markowitz portfolio collapses; and on a real universe of US
sector ETFs the design-regularized portfolio cuts turnover roughly tenfold versus
sample Markowitz while quadrupling effective breadth, at comparable risk-adjusted
return.
\end{abstract}
